For the parent who never expected this road

You may not have chosen the journey.
You can still choose how you travel it.

When a child is diagnosed with a disability, families are suddenly asked to learn a language of medical terms, school systems, accommodations, therapies, advocacy, and a future that may look different from the one they imagined. This book is about finding your footing, trusting your child, and becoming the advocate they need while still allowing them to become the person they were meant to be.

“We weren’t just raising our daughter. She was raising us.”
Who this book is for

You are not the only one learning as you go.

1

Parents at the beginning

For families processing a diagnosis and wondering what comes next—emotionally, practically, and educationally.

2

Parents becoming advocates

For caregivers learning to navigate schools, services, accommodations, IEPs, 504 plans, and systems that do not always make the path easy.

3

Anyone supporting a family

For educators, relatives, professionals, and friends who want to better understand the realities, fears, growth, and victories behind the journey.

Inside the journey

More than a memoir. A companion for the road ahead.

Kim shares the lessons that came from raising a daughter who is blind—from early medical uncertainty and school advocacy to independence, college, leadership, and learning when to step in and when to step back.

✓
Advocate with confidence.
Learn how to ask questions, challenge assumptions, and push for the support your child needs.
✓
Protect without limiting.
Explore the difficult balance between keeping your child safe and giving them room to grow.
✓
Prepare your child to speak up.
See why self-advocacy, independence, and high expectations matter from an early age.
✓
Make space for your own emotions.
Recognize that fear, frustration, pride, grief, joy, and hope can all exist in the same story.

About the author

Kim Alfonso, MBA

Kim Alfonso is a business leader, disability advocate, accessibility professional, and—most importantly for this story—a mother who learned to navigate the unexpected alongside her daughter.

Her experience raising a child who is blind shaped not only how she parented, but how she thinks about inclusion, independence, opportunity, and the importance of creating a world where people with disabilities can participate fully.

In We’re Off on a Journey Few Understand, Kim shares the lessons she wishes more families had access to when their own journey begins.
